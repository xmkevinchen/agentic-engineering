Discuss: Three Rounds and a Loop

Three rounds against three seats, and a loop that re-enters at the first. What each round buys that the one before it cannot, which seats qualify and why none of them outranks another, and where the family boundary actually falls.

Precondition

When the stage runs at all

/ae:go invokes this stage once per id in the analysis frontmatter’s discuss: list. An empty list is a decision the analysis already made — nothing was contested — and the entry does not second-guess it into a discussion. No id, no spawn, no cost.

Each run settles exactly one id, and it settles the question written under that heading rather than a neighbouring one that looks more interesting. Its record is named for that id — decision-<id>.md — which is the only fixed thing about the file. The name carries control flow: it says which question a record answers, and it makes a record that was never written visible, because the ids in the analysis minus the files on disk is the work still outstanding. A question that ran and was sent back leaves a second name — returned-<id>.md — so the directory tells apart three states that would otherwise look alike: settled, returned, and never started. That one is a reject tag rather than a record: it carries no evidence of its own, and ANALYZE deletes it once it has acted.

Mechanism

Three rounds, a loop, and seats that never address each other

The main session never leaves. It relays every round, decides, and writes the record; the seats supply what it could not have produced alone. The ordering is the whole point — a seat that sees your answer first returns a reflection of your draft — and the three are a default path rather than a pipeline: each round ends by reading what came back and choosing the next move.

Every round asks for something the one before it cannot give. The first buys answers that are uncorrelated; the second buys correction against fact rather than agreement; the third is the last point at which the deciding session’s own premises can still be attacked, because by then the composite is what is about to be written down.

Nothing is relayed over a live edge between seats, and that is not only a matter of independence. Any such edge is only as long-lived as the shortest backend session behind it: in the one run that tried it, a thirty-minute session TTL expired inside the gap between two exchanges and the edge was dead before it was used, silently. Relaying through the main session has no such lifetime. Nothing is resumed across rounds either: each round spawns its seats fresh and the main session injects what the last round wrote. A seat is carried forward by its output, not by its own history — which is the disk hand-off rule the workflow already runs on, applied one level down.

Selection

Two tests, not a priority list

A seat qualifies on two counts that do not substitute for each other. A different prior is what the stage is buying. Enough capability to produce an objection you can check is the floor beneath it — and being unlike you does not redeem a backend that answers with the generic benefits of whatever it was asked about. That reading costs real attention and leaves nothing to argue with.

Ranking the seats in a fixed order gets this wrong in a specific way: it puts a weak local backend ahead of a capable agent of your own family, purely for being foreign. There is no such ranking. Take whoever clears both tests, and more than one where you can.

Where there was no outside party at all, the record says that. A decision made by one party is still a decision — it is one whose blind spot is unrecorded, and a record that hides which kind it is gets sent back.

The family question

The seat is Claude. The voice is not.

Yes — agents of the main session’s own family take part, in three distinct roles, and only one of them is a voice in the argument. Conflating them is what makes “cross-family” sound like more than it is.

same family · decides

The main session

Argues its own side, relays every round, chooses, and writes the record. The decision was never delegated — the seats correct it, they do not settle it.

same family · foreign voice

The proxy seats

Claude processes that relay. Substituting their own reasoning for the backend’s, or reporting a comparison the backend did not make, is the failure they are written to prevent.

same family · own voice

The same-family seat

A fresh-context agent of your own family, seated alongside the others rather than behind them. It brings capability you can count on and clean context, and fails only the prior — which the record names. On the evidence so far, the sharpest objection any exchange produced came from one of these.

Host mechanics

What the spawn actually creates

Whether an agent is given a name decides what it becomes. With agent teams enabled a named subagent launches as a teammate — with a mailbox, a place in the sibling roster, and a nesting ban of its own. The seats are left unnamed on purpose: the independence round ① depends on then follows from the wiring rather than from anything a prompt asks for.

FactConsequence for this stage
AGENT_TEAMS=1 A named subagent launches as a teammate rather than a plain subagent. That is why the seats are deliberately unnamed — naming does not merely add an address, it changes the agent’s runtime class.
unnamed An unnamed spawn stays an ordinary subagent: no mailbox, absent from the sibling roster, and its answer returns as the Agent call’s own result rather than over a team channel. Its effort receipt therefore arrives in-band, in the reply itself.
no resume Every round spawns fresh. What carries forward is the previous round’s written output, injected by the main session — not a seat’s own history. No backend session has to survive the gap between rounds, and a failed round is re-run rather than recovered.
model The spawn prompt’s model beats the agent definition’s. A seat may therefore run on something other than the model its own file declares.
skills A definition’s skills: is not applied to a teammate in either display mode. Anything a seat must know belongs in its body or its spawn prompt.
Cost. Two seats through two rounds, then a close-out from four separate angles — eight agent turns for one contested question, and more whenever a close-out sends the composite back for another pass. That is the budget, and it buys one thing: a correction, an option, or an objection the deciding session could not have produced alone. It does not buy consensus. Nothing here converges by counting, because agreement raises nothing when two families can share a blind spot.

Falsifiability

Why the record names names

Every option and every objection carries one clause saying which party produced it. That attribution is not bookkeeping — it is the only thing that will ever settle whether this section earns its cost.

  • Across enough features, it can be read off whether an outside party ever changed a decision, or ever corrected one.
  • If the answer turns out to be never, the right move is to delete this whole mechanism — and there will be evidence for doing it.
  • Until then, an unattributed record is a claim that the seats helped, with nothing behind it.
